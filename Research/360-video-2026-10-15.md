## Convention (EquirectProjection, tested)

COLMAP's EQUIRECTANGULAR camera (cameras.bin model 17, no parameters): panorama frame x right, y DOWN, z forward; lon = atan2(x, z), lat = asin(y/|d|);
u = W (0.5 + lon / 2pi), v = H (0.5 + lat / pi). `view_from_pano` rows = the view's right (horizontal), down, forward axes. Ring10 = 8 horizon views 45 degrees
apart (90 degree field, half overlap) + up + down. Five tests (inverse, axes, ViewRotation vs an independent pitch-then-yaw derivation, rendered directions,
and rolling the panorama half a turn = turning the view 180 degrees with WHITE NOISE and an ODD view size - the only one of them that fails if the seam
wrap becomes a clamp; the direction panorama is continuous across the seam and even sizes never put a pixel centre near it).

## Result: the existing SfM recovers the panorama poses essentially exactly

`tools/equirect_to_views.py` (12 wide-baseline frames x ring10, 1024 px = 120 views) -> the unchanged user path (`AUTOTEST=project`, `&exportcolmap=1`) ->
`tools/compare_pano_poses.py` (pano_from_world = view_from_pano^T view_from_world per placed view; chordal mean per frame; relative rotations between frames,
which do not depend on the arbitrary world frame; centres after a similarity fit):

- 93 of 120 views placed (all 8 horizon views in 9 of 12 frames, 7 in the other 3); NOT placed: all 24 up/down views (sky; rocks and surf under a moving drone).
- Rig consistency (views of one frame vs their mean): 0.10-0.72 degrees.
- Relative rotation vs COLMAP's spherical reconstruction over 66 frame pairs: **median 0.11 deg, max 0.23 deg**.
- Camera centres after a similarity fit: RMS 0.007 on a ~6-7 unit extent (~0.1%).
- Trained 3000 iterations on the 93 views: supervised PSNR 27-32 dB per view (usable photographs; not a held-out score).

So the convention is right (a flipped axis would be tens of degrees off) and 360 needs no new SfM for this kind of footage - conversion is the work.

4. Capture guidance (their tips: stick two-thirds out, slow steady walk, 1 m from surfaces and never under 30 cm, perimeter then grid then close the loop, even light, 1/500 shutter indoors).
